TalkByte AI — User Research Plan

Both segments · Interviews + Survey · 1-week sprint · Aug 2026
🎯 Research Plan
What we're trying to learn before writing a line of code
Objective

Validate the 6 critical assumptions behind TalkByte — particularly willingness to pay, AI acceptance, and whether WhatsApp beats SMS for payment flows in the AU market — before committing to Sprint 1 architecture.

Participants

5–6 Restaurant owners (you have contacts — prioritise these). They are your paying customer; their willingness to pay is the most critical assumption.

5 End customers who regularly order by phone. Recruit via restaurant owner referrals.

Methods

Interviews (qual): 30-min Zoom/in-person, 5–6 owners + 5 customers. Use guides below.

Survey (quant): 15 questions distributed via your restaurant contacts. Ask each owner to forward to their regulars. Target 50+ responses in 5 days.

📋 Restaurant Owner Screener
Use this to qualify owners before booking an interview — 5 quick questions
1. What type of restaurant do you run? (cuisine, format — dine-in / takeaway / delivery)
✅ Pass: Any format that takes phone orders (most will)
2. Roughly how many phone orders do you receive on a typical weekday? And on a Friday or Saturday?
✅ Pass: 5+ calls/day on weekdays, 15+ on weekends
❌ Disqualify: "We don't really do phone orders" — they have no pain
3. Do you have staff dedicated to answering the phone, or does the kitchen handle it?
✅ Pass: Kitchen staff or owner personally answers — high pain
4. Are you the owner or do you make decisions about the tools the restaurant uses?
✅ Pass: Owner or manager with budget authority
❌ Disqualify: "I just work here" — not the buyer
5. Would you be comfortable spending 30 minutes on a Zoom or in person this week?
✅ Pass: Yes
🏪 Restaurant Owner Interview Guide
30 minutes · Record with permission · Prioritise listening over following the script exactly
⏱ Warm-up (5 min) — Build rapport, set context
W1
Tell me about your restaurant — how long have you been open, what type of food, roughly how many covers a night?
Just context-setting. Don't rush past this — it informs everything else.
W2
How do orders currently come in? Walk me through the different channels — in-person, phone, apps like Uber Eats, your own website.
Listen for the mix. Phone orders as a % matters a lot.
🔥 Phone Ordering Pain (10 min) — Current workflow deep dive
P1
Walk me through exactly what happens when the phone rings during a busy service. Who picks up? What do they have to stop doing?Critical
Listen for: kitchen staff interrupted, orders written on paper, miscommunication, waiting on hold.
P2
Has your phone ever rung and no one was able to answer it? What happened?
If yes: How often does that happen? / Do you know how many orders you've lost?
P3
What's the most frustrating thing about managing phone orders right now?
Let them vent. The language they use will become your marketing copy.
P4
Have you ever had an order come in wrong because of a miscommunication on the phone? What happened?
Probe for frequency. This is your "cost of the problem" signal.
💰 Willingness to Pay (10 min) — Most critical section
$1
If a tool could handle every phone order automatically — the customer calls, AI takes the order, sends them a payment link, and pushes the order straight to your kitchen — what would that be worth to you per month?Critical
Do NOT anchor with a price first. Let them name a number. Then: "What if it cost $X/month — would that still make sense?"
$2
Would you prefer to pay a flat monthly fee, or a small fee per order handled?
Per-order models can feel safer for owners but hurt our margins. Understand the instinct.
$3
What would need to be true for you to sign up without a second thought?
Listen for: free trial, no contract, proof it works, demo call.
$4
What would make you never try it?
Capture exact objections — these become your onboarding risk mitigations.
🖥️ Concept Reaction (8 min) — Show the prototype / flow diagram
C1
Here's how TalkByte works: [show the restaurant dashboard + call flow diagram]. What's your first reaction?
Don't explain too much before they react. Silence is data.
C2
Walk me through this onboarding process — what steps feel straightforward? Where would you get stuck?Critical
Watch for hesitation on: menu upload, Stripe Connect, phone number forwarding.
C3
What's your biggest concern about an AI talking to your customers?
Common: "What if it messes up the order?" / "Customers will think it's rude." / "My regulars like talking to us."
💬 Payment Flow (5 min) — SMS vs WhatsApp
PY1
How do your customers currently pay for phone orders? Cash on pickup? Card at the door? Credit card over the phone?WhatsApp test
This tells us the current baseline we're changing.
PY2
If after ordering by phone, the customer got a payment link via SMS — do you think most of your customers would use it?
What types of customers might not? (older, less smartphone-savvy?)
PY3
Do many of your regular customers use WhatsApp? Would you say most of them prefer WhatsApp over regular texts?WhatsApp test
If yes: "Would a WhatsApp payment link feel more trustworthy to them than an SMS?"
🎁 Wrap-up (2 min)
Z1
Is there anything about managing phone orders that I didn't ask about that you think I should know?
Z2
Do you know any other restaurant owners who deal with this problem? Would you be comfortable introducing me?
📋 End Customer Screener
Recruit via restaurant owner referrals — ask each owner to introduce 2–3 of their regulars
1. How often do you order food from a local restaurant — not Uber Eats/Menulog, but calling directly?
✅ Pass: At least 2–3 times per month
❌ Disqualify: "I only use apps" — they're not the pain point we're solving
2. How do you usually pay when you pick up a phone order?
✅ Pass: Cash or card at pickup — helps us understand the payment baseline
3. Have you ever called a restaurant and been put on hold, or the line was busy?
✅ Pass: Yes — they've experienced the problem firsthand
4. Are you comfortable doing a 20-minute Zoom call this week?
✅ Pass: Yes
📞 End Customer Interview Guide
20 minutes · Focus on their existing habits and AI acceptance · Don't pitch the product
⏱ Warm-up (3 min)
W1
Tell me about how you order food — do you go out, order delivery apps, or call restaurants directly? What's your typical week look like?
W2
When do you actually pick up the phone and call a restaurant instead of using an app?
Listen for: knowing the restaurant personally, menu questions, wanting to customise an order, trust issues with apps.
🔍 Current Phone Ordering Experience (8 min)
E1
Tell me about the last time you called a restaurant to order food. Walk me through exactly what happened.
Don't summarise for them — let them narrate. The details matter.
E2
Have you ever been put on hold when calling to order? Or the line was busy? What did you do?
Did they: hang up and try again? Order somewhere else? Use an app instead?
E3
Has an order ever been wrong because of a misunderstanding on the phone? What happened?
How did that affect your trust in that restaurant?
🤖 AI Acceptance (7 min) — Most critical section
AI1
I'm going to describe a scenario. You call your favourite local restaurant, and an AI system answers — it sounds natural, understands your order perfectly, and confirms everything correctly. How do you feel about that?Critical
Do NOT show any reaction to their answer. Just listen. Follow up: "Tell me more about that."
AI2
What would make you trust that AI? What would make you hang up immediately?
Listen for: natural-sounding voice, speed of response, ability to handle modifications, knowing if it's AI vs human.
AI3
If the AI got your order perfectly right — the right items, the right modifications — would you care that it wasn't a real person?
This is the key acceptance question. Watch for nuance — some will say yes but mean "only if it's good."
AI4
The restaurant lets you know upfront: "Hi, you've reached [restaurant name]. Our AI ordering system will take your order." Does being told upfront it's AI change anything?
Disclosure preference matters for our greeting script design.
💬 Payment Flow (5 min) — SMS & WhatsApp
PY1
After you phone in an order, how do you usually pay? Card at the counter? Cash? Do you ever pay in advance?
PY2
After you place your order, you get a text message with a secure payment link — like the ones you'd get from a parking app. You tap it, enter your card, and you're done. How does that sound?Critical
What concerns you about this? / Would you be worried it's a scam?
PY3
Do you use WhatsApp? Is it something you check regularly throughout the day?WhatsApp test
PY4
If the payment link came through WhatsApp instead of SMS — from the restaurant's WhatsApp — would that feel more or less trustworthy to you? Why?WhatsApp test
Key insight: WhatsApp has a verified business profile + green tick — does that help trust?

Is there anything about calling to order food that I didn't ask about that matters to you?

💬 WhatsApp Payment Flow — Deep Test
Australia-specific assumption: WhatsApp penetration is growing fast in multicultural communities (Vietnamese, Indian, Chinese, Lebanese, Greek) — who also run and frequent many AU independent restaurants
🟢 Why this matters for TalkByte
If WhatsApp converts better than SMS for payment links in AU — and the data suggests it might in multicultural communities — the Phase 1 architecture should support WhatsApp Business API as the primary payment channel, not SMS. This is a cheap assumption to test now but expensive to change after you've built SMS-first. One question in every interview will tell you which channel to build first.
🔑 What to learn from owners
  • → Do their customers already contact them via WhatsApp?
  • → Do they have a WhatsApp Business account already?
  • → Would they trust a payment sent via WhatsApp more than SMS?
  • → Do they serve a predominantly multicultural customer base?
🔑 What to learn from customers
  • → Is WhatsApp a daily app or occasional app for them?
  • → Have they ever transacted money via WhatsApp?
  • → Does the green verified business tick increase trust?
  • → Would they prefer WhatsApp or SMS for a payment link?
Decision Framework — What the research tells you to do
FindingArchitecture decision
70%+ of customers prefer WhatsApp over SMS for paymentBuild WhatsApp Business API integration in Sprint 2 before SMS. Use Twilio WhatsApp or 360dialog.
50–70% prefer WhatsAppBuild SMS in Sprint 2, WhatsApp as add-on in Sprint 3. Make channel configurable per restaurant.
Under 50% prefer WhatsAppSMS is correct for Phase 1. WhatsApp can wait for Phase 2 enterprise features.
Owners already have WhatsApp BusinessStrong signal — add WhatsApp as a first-class integration, not an afterthought.

💡 Jobs to Be Done Framework
Before the interviews, write down your hypotheses. After, update them with what you actually heard.
Hypothesised Jobs (validate or rewrite after interviews)
Restaurant Owner Job #1
When my phone rings during Friday dinner service and my chef has to stop what they're doing to answer it, I want to never miss a phone order without disrupting my kitchen, so I can increase revenue without adding staff or stress.
Restaurant Owner Job #2
When I'm trying to run a tight ship with 3 staff on a Saturday night, I want to have phone orders flow into my POS automatically like Uber Eats does, so I can stop writing orders on paper and re-entering them.
Customer Job #1
When I call my favourite local restaurant and no one picks up or I'm put on hold, I want to place my order quickly without waiting or calling back, so I can pick up dinner without the hassle.
Customer Job #2
When I want to customise an order more than an app allows, I want to explain it to someone (or something) that actually understands, so I can get exactly what I want without settling.
After the research, answer these 5 questions before writing code:
  1. What is the single biggest pain restaurant owners described in their own words?
  2. What is the WTP range you're confident in — and does it support our pricing model?
  3. What is the primary AI acceptance condition from customers? (What has to be true for them to accept it?)
  4. Should the Phase 1 payment channel be SMS or WhatsApp?
  5. What is the one thing you didn't know before starting — that changes something in the architecture?
